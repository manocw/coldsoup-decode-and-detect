Summary. We score each payment with a two-stage LightGBM model over ~270 behavioural features computed from the whole week of the payment's own split: who funded the sender and how fast, whether the payment is part of a fast multi-hop trail, whether a sender-receiver pair is a one-off, whether a hub behaves like a scheduled legitimate relay, and how the wallet's behaviour deviates from its own history. A second stage lets each payment borrow the first-stage scores of payments linked to it in time, including two helper models that learn what relays and planted motifs look like. The model goes from the organiser baseline's 29.9 grouped-CV AP to 62.3, and scores 72.6 on the test week. Easy motifs are essentially solved; medium and hard cases sit close to an information limit that we measured directly.

1. Data and what drives the problem

Each split is one synthetic week of an XRPL-style ledger: ~40k payments between ~700 wallets in XRP and issued USD/GBP/EUR tokens from two issuers, some converting currency through liquidity providers. Train has 1,250 suspicious payments (3.1%). Wallets, issuer addresses and dates differ between splits, so features must describe behaviour, never identity. Payments are valued from the delivered amount at the hourly market rate; the sender's real cost is the first executed transfer leg.

Exploratory analysis (with labels and annotation metadata used for analysis and CV grouping only) produced the findings the whole design rests on:

  1. It is about bad motif instances, not bad wallets. Labels are pure per planted scenario (~5-28 payments). 169 suspicious scenarios sit among 1,328 legitimate look-alike scenarios of the same shapes (fan-out, fan-in, cycle, chain, ...). Suspicious senders send mostly legitimate traffic (~5% suspicious), and 535 of 700 wallets touch something suspicious, so blacklists cannot work.
  2. Speed separates the easy cases. Easy motifs complete in 2-14 minutes (a 6-hop cycle in 2.4 min); look-alikes take about an hour. Medium motifs are slowed to 1-3 h and overlap the look-alikes.
  3. One-off relationships mark planted edges. A pair used once in the week is typical of motifs; legitimate commerce repeats 3-7 times at a fixed period and near-constant amount.
  4. Legitimate relays are the largest false-positive source. 12 cohorts of 100 look-alike relay scenarios run hub-and-spoke rounds all week: 998 of the baseline's top-5% false positives. Relay edges repeat tens of hours apart; half of legitimate relays run "backwards" (hub pays out, is refilled later), while all 12 suspicious relays run forwards and pass on ~95% of each hop.
  5. Raw XRPL fields carry no signal (fees uniform over 10/12/15 drops, LastLedgerSequence always +20, SendMax always 1.01× cost, in-ledger order random). Only context matters: timing, counterparties, value flow.
  6. Planted motifs are uniform in time while background traffic is diurnal or in rounds, so quiet periods are enriched for motifs (suspicious and look-alike alike).

2. Methodology

parquet (transactions + transfers + hourly rates)
 -> one row per payment: sender cost (first leg), delivered value, USD at the hourly rate
 -> 8 feature families, each computed on one split's unlabelled week only (cached)
 -> stage 1: LightGBM on train labels
 -> stage 1 helpers: LightGBM on auxiliary targets (relay cohort, planted motif); targets only
 -> stage 2: LightGBM re-scores each payment from its linked neighbours' out-of-fold stage-1 scores
 -> test scores, min-max scaled to [0, 1]

Design rules: features are relative to the wallet and to the split (ratios, ranks, gaps, recurrence), never addresses, absolute dates or labels. Using the whole unlabelled week of the same split as context is allowed by the rules and is what makes the hard, long-history cases visible at all.

4. Model

  • Stage 1: LightGBM, binary log-loss, 400 trees, learning rate 0.03, 31 leaves, ≥20 samples per leaf, 80% row and column subsampling, L2 = 1.0, averaged over 3 seeds; ~270 features.
  • Stage 2 (score sharing): for each payment, max / mean / top-2 mean of the stage-1 scores of (a) the same sender's and (b) the same receiver's payments within 10 min, 1 h and 6 h, (c) the receiver's onward payments, (d) the sender's funding payments in those windows, and (e) its one-off-pair motif group (links within 2, 10, 30 min). A second LightGBM with the same settings uses these plus the stage-1 features. It is trained on out-of-fold stage-1 scores, and its CV is nested, so no payment ever sees a score produced by a model that saw its label.
  • Auxiliary helper targets (final model): stage 1 also trains two helper LightGBMs on the same behavioural features: "is this payment part of a relay cohort?" (~20k positives) and "is it part of a planted non-relay scenario, suspicious or legitimate look-alike?" (~2k positives). Their out-of-fold scores get the same neighbour pooling and feed stage 2, which can then tell "a structure" apart from "a bad structure" with far more examples than the 1,250 suspicious labels. Disclosure: the targets come from the cohort_id, scenario_id and family annotations in train/labels.csv. They are used as training targets only, never as features; at test time nothing from labels.csv is needed.
  • CPU only: ~4 min on a laptop (21 LightGBM fits), peak memory ~1.9 GB. No GPU, external services, pre-trained models or LLMs at runtime.

5. Validation approach

5-fold StratifiedGroupKFold on train, grouped by relay cohort > scenario > payment, so whole planted motifs and whole relay cohorts stay in one fold. Random row splits leak sibling payments of the same motif across folds and, with only ~250 positives in a 20% holdout, move the score by several points from split to split. The metric is the site's AP, also reported per difficulty (easy / medium / hard positives against all negatives). Hyperparameters were checked but barely matter (default 60.1, 15 leaves 60.3, 7 leaves 59.2: within the ~0.5 seed noise), so effort went into features and the two-stage design.

7. Interesting findings

  • The relay features are a false-positive killer. "Does this pair repeat on a schedule?" and "does the hub run forwards?" cut legitimate relays in the top 5% from 998 to 462 and background payments from 206 to 94.
  • Where the remaining errors are (out-of-fold, v1): 754 of 800 easy positives rank in the top 1,250. The negatives that share that range are mostly legitimate look-alike motifs (329), then relays (109) and background (22). Medium positives sit around rank 2,500, behind ~730 relay and ~660 look-alike payments.
  • The look-alikes are adversarially designed. A legitimate bipartite look-alike steps every 6-12 s, faster than the easy suspicious bipartite (~40 s); a legitimate multi-stage look-alike outranks an easy suspicious one moving in 12-second steps. Speed alone cannot separate them; only combinations of context can.
  • Medium is at the information limit. Given the true scenario groupings and 22 whole-scenario statistics (analysis only), a classifier separates medium suspicious from look-alike scenarios at AUC 0.48-0.57, i.e. chance, versus 0.87-0.91 for easy. The only weak signal: medium motifs are slower than their look-alikes (median step 15-18 vs ~9 min, AUC 0.70) and keep more value per hop (0.91-0.96 vs 0.83-0.88).
  • Hard relays likewise. With the true relay scenarios, the only property separating the 12 suspicious relays from 1,188 legitimate ones is direction (all forwards vs half refills, AUC 0.75); size, rounds, counterparties, retention, amounts and conversions are all at AUC ~0.5.
  • Learning "structure" separately from "bad" helps medium cases. The relay/motif helper targets lift medium AP from 6.3 to 8.5 in CV, the only change that moved medium at all.
  • Score sharing helps where motifs are clear. Stage 2 adds +1.5 to +2.3 CV AP, nearly all of it on easy cases: once part of a fast motif is found, its siblings follow.

9. Limitations

  • Medium cases remain hard (AP ~8) and hard cases near random (AP ~1): slowed-down motifs look like their legitimate look-alikes, and we showed this holds even with perfect grouping.
  • 25 "ambiguous" hard payments sit inside regular scheduled series with the same amount and period as their legitimate siblings: unlearnable by construction.
  • Distribution drift: test has more tiny payments (27% under $20 vs 22%) and more conversions. We prefer ratio and retention features over absolute amounts for that reason.
  • Whole-week per-wallet aggregates could memorise wallets inside train and so inflate CV; wallets are disjoint across splits, so this cannot leak into test (and the site score is above CV).
  • The data is synthetic: the patterns are the simulator's. Transfer to the real XRPL is untested.
  • Future work: a temporal edge-level graph neural network. Stage 2 is already one hand-built round of message passing; we did not expect a GNN to recover the medium/hard signal that whole-scenario statistics lack, and it needs a GPU we did not have.

10. Reproducibility

Python 3.14, CPU only. Put the organiser data under data/train/ and data/test/, then:

python -m venv venv && ./venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt
./venv/bin/python -m src.predict --features starter funding relays fields motifs hops amounts history \
    --seeds 3 --stack --aux --name v2c_8fam_stack_aux   # -> submissions/v2c_8fam_stack_aux.csv

Add --cv for the nested grouped CV report. Libraries: pandas, numpy, pyarrow, scikit-learn, LightGBM, networkx, scipy. Labels and annotation metadata are used only as training targets (main and helper models) and for CV grouping, never as features; no external data.
